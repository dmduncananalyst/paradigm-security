Temporary Security Guard Coverage for Holiday Business Closures

A holiday closure changes the normal building routine

Commercial offices and mixed use properties often operate with fewer tenants, shorter hours, and altered delivery schedules around holidays. A building that normally relies on staff presence may have fewer people available to notice an open door or an unexpected visitor. The right response starts with reviewing the actual closure plan.

Confirm who will be on site and when

Property managers should identify tenant departure times, expected vendors, authorized deliveries, cleaning schedules, and emergency contacts. Different suites may close on different days. A temporary coverage plan should reflect these details rather than assume the building is completely vacant.

Define what security personnel should do

An assignment can specify access monitoring, visitor procedures, patrol locations, incident reporting, and communication with property management. If the property needs vehicle patrol, armed coverage, or another specialized service, that requirement should be discussed explicitly. Security personnel should work within applicable licensing requirements and agreed duties.

Make sure building systems still work

Locks, lighting, alarms, cameras, and emergency contact lists should be reviewed independently of staffing decisions. A guard cannot compensate for every failed system or unsafe condition. Coordination with building operations is especially important when maintenance contacts are away.

Plan early enough to confirm availability

Short notice requests may be harder to staff during holiday periods. Sharing the site address, coverage dates, hours, access instructions, and requested duties allows a security provider to evaluate the assignment. No security arrangement guarantees that theft or other incidents will not occur.
