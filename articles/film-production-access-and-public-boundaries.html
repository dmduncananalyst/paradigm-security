Film Production Access and the Boundary Between Private and Public Space

A filming location may contain a private set, a permitted area on public property and neighboring spaces that remain open to the public. Those boundaries matter. Production security can support an authorized location assignment, but a production's preference for privacy does not automatically give its guards control over every sidewalk, roadway or nearby viewpoint.

Professional boundaries support the production

Productions have legitimate interests in their working environment, equipment and authorized access. Respecting the boundary of an assignment helps protect those interests without creating confusion for residents, pedestrians or adjacent businesses. The Film Commission's guidance emphasizes approvals for activities affecting public streets and traffic. Such matters cannot be resolved merely by hiring additional private guards.

A assessment with Paradigm about film production security should identify the location and the scope of the requested assignment. Permit authority and property permission remain separate issues that the production must address with the appropriate parties. The result should be a clear understanding of what security supports and where the production has permission to operate. That protects the working relationship with the location as well as the surrounding community.
