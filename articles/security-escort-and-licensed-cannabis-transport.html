Security Escorts and Licensed Cannabis Transportation in California

A security company offering escort support is not necessarily a cannabis distributor. These are separate professional roles governed by different requirements. A business moving commercial cannabis between licensed operations needs to understand who is authorized to transport the goods and what role, if any, the accompanying security personnel are performing.

Transportation requires the correct cannabis authority

DCC explains that a distribution license is required for cannabis transportation within the distribution framework. Its license type guidance distinguishes a Type 11 distributor from a Type 13 transport only distributor. These descriptions concern cannabis business authorization, rather than the credentials of a guard accompanying a trip.

The difference matters when a service is described publicly. A statement that a company provides cannabis security does not establish that the company holds a distribution license. A statement that a distributor is licensed does not establish the qualifications of every person accompanying its vehicle. A customer needs accurate role descriptions rather than a single broad label that obscures who is doing what.

An accompanying guard does not become the distributor

DCC's transport personnel provision permits qualifying security personnel to be present in a cannabis transport vehicle alongside the specified authorized persons. Permission to accompany the shipment is not the same as a distribution license. The transportation rules continue to assign obligations to the licensed distributor.

This distinction prevents a common misunderstanding: the presence of security does not convert an otherwise unauthorized transport arrangement into an authorized one. The driver, transporting business and shipment must be evaluated under the applicable cannabis framework. A guard's professional role supports an assignment within that framework instead of replacing the business authorization that governs the goods.

Consumer delivery is a different activity

DCC's license guidance distinguishes distribution from delivery to a consumer. A transport only distribution license does not authorize retail delivery. Using the word delivery loosely can therefore create confusion about an assignment that actually involves movement between licensed businesses.

For example, a business describing a transfer to another licensed premises is reviewing a different transaction from a retailer fulfilling a customer's order. Calling both activities transport does not make the governing license identical. The intended activity needs to be identified clearly enough that a business does not mistake escort support, distribution and retail delivery for the same service.

Public claims should match verified credentials

Cannabis businesses should be cautious about descriptions that imply an escort provider owns the goods, acts as the distributor or carries out every regulated function. Those claims require a factual basis. Private security credentials concern security work; cannabis business credentials concern authorized commercial cannabis activities. BSIS registration and DCC licensing serve different regulatory purposes.

The agreement between the parties should explain the role provided and avoid suggesting that security support removes another participant's compliance duties. A business needs to know who is supplying the security personnel and who is responsible for the regulated shipment. Particular arrangements belong in a private assessment among those parties, rather than an assumption based on a broad service label.

When reviewing cannabis transport support with Paradigm, the business should identify the regulated activity and the authorized parties. Paradigm's role should be stated only to the extent it is supported by verified credentials and an agreed assignment. Clear responsibility is the point of the assessment: the security escort supports the movement, while the required cannabis authorization remains with the properly licensed operator.
